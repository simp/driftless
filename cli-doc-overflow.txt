== pupgrade compile

Writes under the catalogs directory:
- <certname>/catalog.json (Puppet's catalog data, sorted) and
- compile.json (classes and where they were added, functions, facts, lookups, providers, warnings, deprecations, leaks) 

The table counts the how many mocked command and fact executions ran, 
and how many unmocked command and fact executions "leaked".
leaks can by handled as: raised, allowed to run, or a fact missing

- Unanswered commands are logged as warnings.
Exits 1 when a compile fails; its error is listed under the table and kept in <certname>/error.txt.


== pupgrade lookup

The node is built as `compile` builds it: facts and trusted facts from the factset,
$server_facts, and the variables and classes the ENC stand-in decides. Without
--compile no manifest is evaluated, as `puppet lookup` does; with it the catalog is
compiled first, mocks and guard in force, and the lookup runs in its top scope.
Exits 1 when the key is not found.

== pupgrade surface

A module is a directory, or a name resolved on the control repo's modulepath like
`module gitlog` does; `stdlib:v9.6.0` reads a ref of the deployed clone. 

Documents land in the cache as <module>-<version>--<tag>__<sha>.json, keyed on the commit read
(the tag is for the eye; a module that is no git checkout is keyed on its version alone),
and are reused until --refresh, or until pupgrade's document schema moves on, when they are
extracted again as read.

Loading a module's Ruby is how its functions, types, providers, and facts are read, so
extraction runs in a process of its own, with the repo's modulepath in reach. 

Files that fail to load are listed under "errors" in the document and warned about. The
document also carries what metadata.json declares; `module diff` compares two.
